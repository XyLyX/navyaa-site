I've been sitting with this feeling for weeks now. Maybe months. It's not the kind of sadness that has a clear cause—no single event I can point to and say, "That's where it all went wrong." It's more like waking up one day and realising the contract I thought I had with life was never actually signed.

You know how we're raised? We're fed this quiet agreement: work hard, sacrifice, endure the hard parts, and eventually things will balance out. Pain is supposed to be an investment. Suffering builds character. Blood, sweat, tears—they're all down payments on some future peace we've been promised.

But what happens when you've paid and paid, and the hands come back completely empty?

**The Blood I've Given**

I think about everything I've poured into this existence. The late nights working on things that didn't matter. The relationships I gave pieces of myself to, only to watch them dissolve. The dreams I chased until my lungs burnt, only to have them slip through my fingers. The years I spent believing that if I just tried hard enough, I was good enough, loved enough—something would come back to me.

And now? I look at my open palms and there's nothing there. Not even the ghost of what I gave.

It's not that I expected life to be easy. I wasn't naive. But I did expect some kind of reciprocity. Some acknowledgement that my effort counted for something. Instead, I feel like I've been bleeding into a void that doesn't even have the decency to echo back.

**The False Gods I've Worshipped**

Here's the thing that hits hardest: I've spent so much time bowing down to the wrong altars.

I gave my devotion to careers that didn't care if I lived or died. I placed my faith in people who were just as lost as I was. I chased status, validation, and security—all these towering idols that looked so solid from a distance. Up close? They're hollow. Fragile. Blind to my pain.

And the worst part? I watched others do the same. We're all out here kneeling before these paper gods, trading our souls for something that was never going to save us.

I've seen powerful people who demand more worship than any deity ever could. I've watched institutions crush the very people who built them. I've participated in systems that took everything and gave back crumbs, and I called it "success".

Now I'm standing in the rubble of those altars, wondering what I was so afraid of losing.

**The Season That Never Came**

They tell you spring always comes. That after winter, after the long dark, things will green again. New beginnings. Fresh starts. Hope, renewed.

But what if spring arrives and everything is still dead?

I look around and all I see is yellow. Decay. The colour of illness and endings. Even the seasons meant for recovery feel poisoned. The sun comes out, and it feels like mockery. The flowers bloom, and I want to ask them: \*What are you so happy about?\*

When your inner landscape is this devastated, external renewal doesn't feel like hope. It feels like gaslighting. Like the universe is saying, "Look at all this beauty," while you're standing there wondering why you can't feel any of it.

**The Stubborn Child in My Chest**

Here's the part I don't know how to explain to people: I can't settle.

My heart has this childlike rigidity that refuses to compromise. It doesn't want partial fulfilment. It doesn't want crumbs. It doesn't want to celebrate small victories while the big ones remain unreachable.

It's all or nothing.

And I know—intellectually—that this is "unhealthy". That life requires compromise. That maturity means accepting less than what you dreamed. That gratitude for small things is the key to happiness.

But my soul isn't listening.

Confronted with a broken world and empty promises, my heart would rather have nothing at all than accept a comforting illusion. If life can't give me authentic meaning, real connection, and true fulfilment—then I don't want the cheap substitute. I'd rather stand in the void and name it for what it is.

**Standing Before the Universe**

I'm not speaking from petty disappointment. I'm not upset about a bad breakup or a failed project or a rough patch. This is deeper. This is existential burnout.

I'm standing on the absolute floor of what a person can feel.

And I'm looking at the universe with empty hands, and I'm saying, '**You took everything. You took my blood, my youth, my passion, my hope. And what did you give back?'**

Silence.

**What Do You Do With This?**

I don't have a neat answer. I don't have a five-step plan for recovering from this kind of disillusionment. I'm not going to tell you that everything happens for a reason, or that the pain will make you stronger, or that you just need to change your perspective.

Sometimes pain is just pain. Sometimes the ledger doesn't balance. Sometimes you give everything you have and end up with nothing but empty lines on your palms.

But here's what I'm learning: when you stop expecting life to be fair, you stop bargaining with a system that was never keeping score. When you have nothing left to lose, the desperate pleading finally stops. And in that silence—total, absolute, terrifying—there's a strange kind of freedom.

The heart stops begging.

And it begins to exist on its own terms.

I don't know where this ends. I don't know if the spring will ever actually green again, or if I'll always see yellow. But I know I'm done pretending. I'm done with the toxic positivity, the spiritual bypassing, and the relentless pressure to "look on the bright side".

I paid my blood. I have nothing to show for it. And that's the truth.

Some truths don't lead to healing. They don't offer comfort or solutions. They just **are**. And sometimes, the bravest thing you can do is stand in that truth and refuse to look away.

My hands are empty.

But at least I'm not lying about it anymore.
