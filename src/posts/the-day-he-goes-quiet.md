*A personal essay, from the inside of a silence no one asked about.*

There's a version of me you used to know. He argued. He explained. He stayed up late writing messages he rewrote ten times, trying to find the words that would finally land. He apologised for things he didn't do just to end the war. He believed — genuinely, stubbornly believed — that if he could just say it right, you would understand.

I miss that guy sometimes. He had more hope than I do.

## It isn't a decision

Here's the truth no one tells you about the day a man goes quiet: it isn't a decision. It's a slow erosion. It's water wearing down stone, except the stone is you, and one morning you wake up hollowed out and you can't even remember when the last bit washed away.

The tiredness isn't physical. I sleep fine now. Better than I have in years, actually. The tiredness lives somewhere deeper — a bone-tired of the soul.

I'm tired of explaining the same thing five different ways, watching it get twisted into something I never said. I'm tired of apologising for existing in a way that inconveniences someone else's narrative. I'm tired of the moment right after I apologise — that moment where I look up and see it didn't matter anyway, because the apology was never the point. The point was watching me shrink.

## What the anger was

I used to get angry. God, I used to burn. And for a long time I thought that anger was my enemy. I tried to kill it, meditate it away, breathe through it. Now I understand: that anger was proof I still cared. Anger is what love looks like when it's being mishandled. You don't rage at people who mean nothing to you.

The anger is mostly gone now. Not because I healed. Because I stopped caring.

That's the part that scares me.

## When the truth is negotiable

There's a specific kind of loneliness that comes from being in a relationship where the truth is negotiable. Where facts are just weapons, and whoever cares less wins. I used to bring evidence. I used to scroll up in conversations and quote back exact words, as if accuracy could save us. And every single time, I watched the conversation bend away from the truth into something else entirely — a trial, with me as the defendant, and the verdict written before I opened my mouth.

They didn't want to understand me. They wanted to be right. And you cannot argue someone out of a position they didn't reason themselves into. You can only exhaust yourself trying.

I've written before about [the person standing far away](/the-gap-between-ideal-and-real/). This is what that distance feels like from the other side.

## So I stopped

Not dramatically. There was no slammed door, no final speech. Those are for people who still believe the other person is listening. I just… stopped. Stopped explaining. Stopped defending. Stopped the long paragraphs at midnight. I let the silence answer, and the silence turned out to be more honest than anything I'd said in years.

People call it giving up. They say it with contempt, like it's a weakness. But they don't know what it costs to keep fighting for something that only you are fighting for. They don't know that the quiet is not surrender — it's [a kind of silent strength](/silent-strength-embracing-inner-pain/), the last act of self-respect.

I'm not saying I'm blameless. I've been wrong. I've been careless. I've hurt people and deserved the pushback. But there's a difference between being held accountable and being held hostage. One builds you. The other just teaches you to stop speaking.

## For the one who went quiet

If you're reading this and you know someone like me — someone who went quiet — don't assume he doesn't care. Assume he cared so much, for so long, that caring became a wound. Assume he's tired. Assume the words were used up before the silence ever started.

And if you're the one who's gone quiet — I see you. You're not cold. You're not broken. You just finally learned that some things can't be explained to people who've already decided what you are. That's not your failure.

That's your release.

I don't know if I'll stay this quiet forever. Part of me hopes not — that the hope comes back, that I find rooms where my words land gently. But until then, I'm not fighting. I'm resting. I'm letting the silence do what my explanations never could:

Protect what's left of me.

*Written from the quiet. Not from the cold.*
