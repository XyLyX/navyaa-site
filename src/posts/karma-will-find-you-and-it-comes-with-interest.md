*A personal reflection on the one debt none of us can escape*

Let's start with what most people get wrong.

Karma is not a cosmic police force. It doesn't send a lightning bolt on schedule. It isn't parked outside the house of the person who wronged you, holding a clipboard. If that's what you're waiting for, you will wait your whole life and be disappointed.

I know, because I waited.

I watched people who lied, cheated, and took credit for other people's work walk away with the title, the bonus, and the applause. I told myself, *It will catch up with them.* Some days I believed it. Most days it sounded like a cope, a bedtime story for people who lost.

Here is what I understand now: **the universe does not owe you a show.** What it runs is a system, and the system is far more certain than revenge.

## Karma Is Not Revenge. It's Accounting.

Revenge is emotional. Accounting isn't.

Every action you take enters a ledger: the lie you told to close a deal, the friend you dropped when they stopped being useful, the person you humiliated because you could, and the promise you made while already planning to break it. None of it is erased. It is recorded in other people's memories, in your habits, in your nervous system, and in your character.

And like any ledger, it compounds.

A small lie doesn't stay small. It needs a second lie to protect it, then a third, until you're maintaining a structure of deceit that demands constant effort and constant fear of exposure. You think you're getting away with it. You are actually *servicing a debt* every single day, paid in peace of mind.

That's the first payment, and it's due immediately.

## The Payment Nobody Sees

We look for karma in dramatic events: the bankruptcy, the divorce, the scandal, and the illness. Sometimes it does arrive that way. But most of the time the payment is quiet, and it starts the moment you act.

**The cheater can't fully trust anyone.** He knows what he's capable of, so he assumes everyone else is capable of it too. He lives in a mind where every smile is a possible trap.

I've sat across the table from business partners who never signed anything without three lawyers, never delegated anything, never slept easy. They'd done to others exactly what they feared would be done to them. Their paranoia wasn't bad luck. It was their own record, read back to them.

**The manipulator never experiences being truly loved.** People respond to the mask, so even real affection feels engineered. She can never be sure it would survive without the performance.

**The ruthless climber ends up surrounded by people exactly like himself.** I've seen leaders who stepped on everyone on the way up reach the top and find it empty. When the climbing stopped, the loyalty stopped too, because loyalty was never what held it together. Transaction was.

**The person who humiliates others lives in constant defence.** Anyone who enjoys power over people has to keep guarding it. That's not freedom. It's a prison with a nicer view.

You don't need a cosmic court to explain this. You become what you repeatedly do. The man who lies for twenty years doesn't just tell lies. He becomes someone who can't recognise the truth, even when it is the only thing that could save him.

That is interest, charged daily, in the one currency you can't earn back: the quality of your inner life.

## The Part That Makes People Angry

Now the uncomfortable truth, the one the motivational posters skip.

**Sometimes the guilty never face a visible reckoning.** Some people die rich, respected, and surrounded by flowers at a funeral where everyone lies about who they were. I've been to those funerals. Anyone who promises you every wrongdoer will be publicly destroyed is selling comfort, not truth.

And the reverse is real too: good people suffer. Honest people lose to dishonest ones. Kind people get used. Some who did everything right still get betrayed and still bury people they love.

So if karma isn't a guaranteed *external* scoreboard, what is it?

It's a guaranteed *internal* one.

**You can escape consequences. You cannot escape yourself.** The man who cheated his partner and kept the house still has to live as the man who cheated his partner. The woman who took the credit and got the promotion still knows she can't do the work. Nobody sentences them. They carry the verdict into every quiet room for the rest of their lives, and people who have never done wrong can't imagine how heavy that room becomes at 3 a.m.

This is why I stopped treating karma as a revenge fantasy. Hoping someone suffers is itself a withdrawal from *your* account. It keeps you chained to them, still handing them your attention, rent-free.

## Why "I'll Balance It Later" Is a Lie

The most dangerous belief is the bargain: *do the wrong now, offset it with something good later.* A donation. A prayer. A burst of generosity after a week of damage.

It doesn't work, for a simple reason: **good deeds don't cancel harm to the person you harmed.**

The woman you humiliated doesn't feel better because you fed strangers last Ramadan. The partner you betrayed doesn't heal because you were generous at a wedding. The employee you cheated of wages isn't compensated by your reputation as a philanthropist.

Harm is specific. It lands on a specific person, at a specific time, in a specific way. Repair has to be specific too. You can't pay off one creditor by depositing money in another's account.

Real repair means facing the person you hurt, owning it without excuses, and changing the behaviour permanently. Most people would rather write a cheque than have that conversation. That's exactly why the debt stays open.

## When It Was Me

I promised myself this essay wouldn't only point fingers, so here is my entry.

There was a time I chose a deadline over a person. Someone who trusted me to back them up, and I let them take the blow instead because it was easier for me. I told myself it was business. I told myself everyone does it.

It took me years to see what it cost. Not the relationship, though that went too. It cost me something I couldn't price at the time: the certainty that I was the kind of person who stands by people. I had to rebuild that from nothing, one uncomfortable decision at a time.

That's the lesson I keep returning to. The consequence of a wrong is rarely what other people do to you. It's what you find out about yourself.

## The Reality of Time

"When will it come back to them?"

Honestly, I don't know. Maybe next month. Maybe thirty years from now. Maybe in a form you'll never witness. Maybe only in the silence of their own mind.

What I do know: **time is not forgiveness.** Delay is not acquittal. Years of apparent success after a wrong are not proof the wrong didn't count. They are the grace period, and grace periods end.

I also know what waiting does to you. If your peace depends on watching someone fall, you've handed your recovery to a stranger's misfortune. Stop checking the clock. Don't be the audience of their downfall. Your job is to stop being their creditor in your own mind and to stop adding entries to your own ledger while you're busy reading theirs.

## So What Do You Do With This?

Not everyone reading this was the one wronged. Be honest: some of you did the wrong.

You know the entries. The apology you never made, the money you never returned, the lie that's still running, the person you quietly erased because facing them was inconvenient.

You can't reverse time, but you can stop the compounding.

* **Stop the harm first.** Not tomorrow. Not after the deal closes. Now.
* **Tell the truth, even where it costs you.** Especially there. The cost is the repayment.
* **Make it right with the actual person.** Not with a bigger audience, not with a gesture. With them.
* **Accept that some people won't forgive you.** That doesn't void your responsibility. It is the consequence, and you carry it with dignity instead of resentment.
* **Change what you do when no one is watching.** That's the only audit that matters.

And if you were the one wronged, grieve it, name it, protect yourself from it happening again, and then close the account in your head. Not because they deserve forgiveness, but because you deserve your life back.

## The Bottom Line

Karma is not magic. It's not a miracle. It's not a promise that villains die screaming and saints die rewarded.

It's something harsher and more reliable: **every action shapes the person who does it, the relationships built around them, and the world they have to live in.** At every moment, you are constructing the life you will be forced to inhabit.

Lie, and you will live among lies. Betray, and you will live suspecting betrayal. Be cruel, and you will live inside the mind of someone cruel. Be honest, and you will sleep. Be loyal, and you will be surrounded by loyalty. Be kind, and you will know what it feels like to be safe among people.

Nobody has to punish you. You will simply have to *be you*, with everything you chose.

So before your next decision, ask one question:

**Can I live with being the person who does this for the rest of my life?**

If the answer is no, don't do it.

The bill is already running. The only question is what's written on it.
