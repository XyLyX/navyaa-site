**Expect Less From People. Demand More From Yourself.**

The people who hurt me most never meant to. They were the ones closest to me.

A stranger breaking a promise is an annoyance. When someone I love does it, it feels like a verdict on how much I matter. I’ve waited by the phone for a call promised "tonight". I’ve kept a seat empty for someone who swore they’d come. I’ve shown up, unasked, for people who weren’t there on the day I needed the same. And every time, the same thought: *I would never do this to them.*

That thought was the problem. It took me far too long to see it.

### The contract nobody signed

With the people I love, I had drawn up contracts they never signed. If I’m there for you, you’ll be there for me. If I remember your birthday, you’ll remember mine. If I give you my full attention, you’ll give me yours. I called it basic decency. It was a set of demands I never said out loud, and I punished people for failing them.

When someone fell short, I never thought “they’re human.” I thought “they don’t care.” Then I carried that for days, sometimes years, over something the other person had forgotten by the next morning.

Here’s what I wouldn’t admit: none of them were living by my rulebook. Some are always late and always will be. Some say “yes” because it’s easier than “I can’t.” Some love you deeply but not in the language you speak. Some are so buried in their own mess that they have nothing left to give, and have no idea you’re running on empty too. People will cancel. They’ll change. They’ll promise things and not do them. That’s not betrayal. That’s human nature, running without your permission.

### Why is your mood in someone else’s hands?

This is the question I should have asked years ago. Why does my mood depend on whether somebody else showed up? Why am I angry that they didn’t work as hard as I did, didn’t keep their word, didn’t care as much as I cared?

Every high expectation I held was a remote control I handed to someone else. One unanswered message and my whole day changed channel. A perfectly good Tuesday, ruined by a person who didn’t even know they’d done it.

I wasn’t really angry at what they did. I was angry at the gap between the movie in my head and what actually happened. Reality hadn’t done anything wrong. I’d written a script, and nobody had read it.

### The one that hurts the most

The hardest version is when someone you’d do anything for won’t do the same for you.

I’ve been that person for others. I’ve dropped everything, given time and money without keeping count. And I’ve met the silence on the other side, when I needed the same and got a polite, distant “hope you’re okay.” For a long time I read that as proof I’d been a fool.

It wasn’t proof of that. It was proof that people give in the ways they’re able to. Someone can be generous in a hundred ways I never notice and unavailable in the one way I needed. When I stopped asking “why didn’t they come through?” and started asking “what can they actually offer?”, something loosened in my chest.

Guess what? It’s fine. It hurts, and it’s fine. Both are true. I can love someone and still stop handing them jobs they never accepted.

### The waiting habit

Here’s the quieter cost nobody mentions. High expectations don’t just make you angry. They train you to wait.

Wait for someone to follow through. Wait for someone to say the idea is good. Wait for someone to carry their half before you move yours. I lost months this way, standing still and resentful, watching for backup from people who never agreed to be my backup.

Nobody was ever going to hand me permission. Waiting wasn’t patience. It was me outsourcing my momentum to people who didn’t know they were holding it.

### Flip the equation

So I flipped it. Low expectations of other people. High expectations of myself.

I’ll say it before you do: this isn’t cynicism. It’s clarity. It strips out the drama and leaves people as they are, not as I wish them to be. And something odd happens when you stop expecting. You start appreciating. An unprompted call feels like a gift. A kept promise lands as warmth, not as a debt finally paid. Expectation turns love into a ledger. Drop it, and love goes back to being generous.

The high standard doesn’t disappear. It just moves inward:

* **If something needs to get done,**

   I’ll figure it out.
* **If life gets difficult,**

   I’ll handle it.
* **If somebody leaves,**

   I’ll grieve if I need to, then keep moving.
* **If nobody believes in what I’m building,**

   I’ll believe enough for all of them.

That last one cost me the most to learn. I spent too long waiting for a “this is a good idea” that no one was ever going to formally give. Now I move first, and let the work do the talking.

### Peace and momentum are on the same side

Once I stopped demanding the world follow my script, two things happened almost immediately.

My peace stopped leaking over things I could never control: who called, who remembered, who showed up. And my momentum stopped stalling while I waited for permission, backup or applause. If someone joins me, wonderful. If not, nothing is lost and nothing stops.

Nobody can mess with your head when your head isn’t on loan.

### What this isn’t

It isn’t permission to be passive, and it isn’t an excuse to tolerate disrespect. Low expectations come with firm boundaries. When someone shows you who they are, believe them, and change your behaviour, not your blood pressure. You can expect nothing from a person and still decide they don’t get your time, your trust or your energy.

And it isn’t an excuse to be sloppy with your own word. The standard I stopped forcing on everyone else is the one I hold myself to. If I say I’ll be there, I’m there. My integrity is the only part of this I fully control, so that’s where I’m strict.

### Where I’ve landed

I draw the line between healthy trust and setting myself up for disappointment with one question: *is my peace depending on this?* If it is, I rebuild the plan. If it isn’t, I enjoy people’s kindness when it shows up and lose nothing when it doesn’t.

Expect less from the world. Demand more from yourself. That way other people can’t mess with your peace, and you never stop pushing forward.

I don’t need everyone to show up. I need to show up for myself, quietly and consistently, without keeping score of who didn’t. That’s a pretty powerful way to live.

*Where do you draw the line between trusting people and setting yourself up for disappointment?*
