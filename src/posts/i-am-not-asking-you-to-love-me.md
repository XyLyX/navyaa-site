**1:04 AM: The Architecture of Unreturned Things**

There is a particular quality to the silence at one in the morning. It does not press in the way daytime silence does—the kind that waits to be interrupted by a notification, a kettle boiling, or the obligation to seem fine. At 1:00 AM, the room is entirely honest. The noise of performance has packed up for the night, and what is left sitting at the edge of the bed is the truth you spent fourteen hours outrunning.

Perhaps you do not love me. Perhaps you never did, and never will, for the obvious reasons.

It is the story of my life. I have said that sentence so many times—rehearsed it in the shower, typed and deleted it in half-lit rooms, murmured it like a rosary against the hope that refuses to die—that it almost sounds calm. It sounds like acceptance. It sounds like wisdom. It is neither. It is just exhaustion wearing a composed face.

When my own language runs out of breath, I always end up borrowing from the dead poets who bled in Urdu so the rest of us wouldn’t have to invent the vocabulary from scratch. Ahmad Faraz said for me what I could never say without my voice breaking:

> *Ranjish hi sahi dil hi dukhane ke liye aa* *Aa phir se mujhe chhod ke jaane ke liye aa* *(Let it be only anguish, come just to break my heart / Come, if only to leave me once again)* — **Ahmad Faraz**

### What I carry

People talk about unrequited love as if it is a lack—an empty cup, an unanswered knock, a hollow space where someone else was supposed to stand. They have it backward. The ache is not from emptiness. The ache is from fullness with nowhere to pour itself.

I am filled to the brim with you.

This love does not wait for permission, and it has not noticed that nothing is coming back. It operates on its own weather system. It wakes before I do, already heavy in my chest before my feet even touch the cold floor. It sits beside me through ordinary hours—through spreadsheets and traffic, through coffee with friends who think I am just tired, through the small talk and the practiced, functional face I wear for other people. I nod, I laugh at the right cues, I answer emails. And underneath all of it, there is this quiet, relentless haemorrhage. Some days, living feels like dying a little and carrying on anyway.

I do not hold it against you that you cannot feel what I feel. Truly, I don't. A heart is not a switchboard; it cannot be ordered into devotion, and guilt is a terrible counterfeit for affection. You are not a villain for being unmoved.

What hurts is not your absence of love. What hurts is the silence around mine—the casualness with which it is treated, as if something this large, this consuming, had landed in the room and left no mark at all. As if a wildfire could burn through an entire forest and the sky above wouldn't even smell of smoke.

### What I ask

At 1:00 AM, pride negotiates its terms down to the barest minimum. I have stopped asking the universe for miracles, and I have stopped asking you for a future.

I am not asking you to love me.

I am asking you to respect what I feel and to know its worth. Do not reduce it to an inconvenience. Do not laugh at it—not openly, and not in the quiet, patronising way people smile at someone who cares too much. Do not treat my devotion as a predictable habit of mine that you have simply learned to step around, like a loose floorboard in a hallway you walk down every day.

What I have given you—silently, without contracts or conditions—is the rarest thing a human being possesses: the entirety of their tenderness. If you cannot hold it, that is your right. Only if you must turn it away, do it gently. Look at what you are declining. Turn it away with your eyes open.

### The pride of love

In that same famous ghazal—the one that begins with *Ranjish hi sahi*—Faraz gives this specific, excruciating wound a name I keep returning to in the dark: *pindar-e-mohabbat*. The pride of love.

Later in the poem, the speaker pleads with the beloved to protect that pride just a little—to be the one who makes the effort, just once, to keep the dignity of the lover intact:

> *Kuch to mere pindar-e-mohabbat ka bharam rakh* *Tu bhi to kabhi mujhko manane ke liye aa* *(Keep at least a little of the dignity of my love / You too, come just once to win me back)*

That is the line that undoes me every single time.

Because I have always been the bridge. I have always been the one who reaches first, who softens the edges of an awkward silence, who explains what I meant so you wouldn't have to wonder, who forgives before an apology is even shaped, and who returns after swearing I wouldn't. When you love someone without return, you strip yourself of every defence mechanism piece by piece until you are standing completely unarmed.

*Pindar*—dignity, self-respect, that fragile spine of pride—is the very last thing left to someone who has given everything else away. And mine asks for so little. It does not ask for forever. It asks for one single moment in which the gravity reverses. One moment in which I am not the one chasing, waiting, or understanding—one moment in which I am the one being sought.

### What remains

The clock ticks over to 1:24 AM. The room hasn't changed, and neither have you.

Maybe that moment will never come. If I am being entirely honest with myself—the kind of honesty only the middle of the night allows—some quiet, grounded part of me already knows it won't. You cannot extract water from stone, no matter how patiently you kneel beside it.

Still, I refuse to be ashamed of the depth of my own capacity to feel. What I felt was real. It was uncalculated, it was generous, and it deserved tenderness.

If you cannot give it that tenderness, then the burden shifts back to me. I will have to learn to gather up all this spilled devotion and pour a little of it back into my own hollow spaces. I will have to learn to be gentle with the part of me that loved you so fiercely for nothing in return.

I do not know how to do that yet. Tonight, it is enough to have stopped pretending I am fine, to have let the silence hear the truth, and to have finally said it aloud.
