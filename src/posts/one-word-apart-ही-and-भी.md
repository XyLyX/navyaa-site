When “You Are My World” Became “You Are Also in the World”

पहले लगता था तुम ही दुनिया हो,
अब ये लगता है तुम भी दुनिया हो।

I read these two lines years too late, and they still stopped me.

One tiny word changes: ही becomes भी. “You alone are my world” becomes “you are also in the world.” It is a difference of a single syllable, and it holds an entire heartbreak.

The Ground Under My Feet

There was a time when I believed in people completely. Not casually, but the way you believe in the ground under your feet. You don’t test the floor each morning before stepping out of bed. You don’t ask whether it will hold. You simply walk.

That was how I trusted. I never questioned whether certain people would be there, so I never built anything to stand on if they weren’t. No second plan, no quiet reserve, no corner of myself kept safe from weather.

That is what devotion does. It makes one person the centre of everything: your plans, your mornings, your sense of safety. The calendar bends around them. Good news isn’t real until it has been told to them. A bad day is survivable only because they exist at the end of it.

From the inside, it doesn’t feel like a risk. It feels like love at its purest. That is exactly why it is so dangerous.

How Reality Arrives

Reality rarely comes with a crash. I used to imagine betrayal as a dramatic scene, a slammed door, a raised voice, a clear before and after. It almost never happens that way.

It comes quietly. A hard year. Money problems. A promise that slips, then another. A crisis that tests everyone in the house, not just you. And in the middle of it, people change.

Some harden. Some retreat. Some stay in the room but are no longer present in it. Their body is at the table while the person you knew has gone somewhere you cannot follow.

I used to think hardship reveals people, that pressure simply strips away the pretence and shows who they always were. Now I think it also rewrites them. Pressure makes some people grow and makes others go cold. Both are real, and both are human.

The hardest thing to accept is that you cannot tell which is which until you are standing in your worst hour, looking for a hand that doesn’t come.

The Quiet Kind of Cold

When a stranger is cold, it’s weather. You shrug, you pull your coat tighter, you move on. It was never about you.

But when someone close turns indifferent, someone who once knew the blueprint of you, it is a different kind of hurt. It isn’t loud. It is:

the look that passes through you rather than at you
the “I’m busy” that slowly becomes a lifestyle
the messages answered late, then briefly, then not at all
the slow realisation that you are carrying a relationship alone

That kind of indifference sucks the life and soul out of you from within. Anger would be easier. Anger at least admits you mattered. It is a form of attention, even of respect. Indifference says something far crueler: you did not register.

And so you keep functioning. You work. You eat. You smile on schedule, answer emails, show up where you are expected. From the outside, nothing looks wrong. But inside there is the stillness of a room where a fire just went out. The warmth fades slowly enough that you notice only when you realise you are cold.

What Grows in the Silence

And yet something grim and useful grows in that silence. Clarity.

You begin to see that the world is far bigger than any one person. That the sky did not shrink when someone stopped standing in it. And you see something harder, something you would rather not look at: you gave one human being the power to be your entire sky, and no human being can carry that weight.

It wasn’t entirely their failure. It was also the cost of loving without a margin. When one person is everything, their bad season becomes your collapse. Their distance becomes your exile. We ask them to be home, country, and compass all at once, and then grieve when they turn out to be only a person.

I say this not to excuse anyone, and not to blame myself. Both can be true: they let go, and I had built a life with a single beam holding up the roof.

ही to भी

The shift from ही to भी is not bitterness. Bitterness would be to say no one is my world, no one can be trusted. That is just the old absolutism wearing a darker coat.

This is something else. It is maturity, bought at a high price. The heart wanted absolutes: always, only, everything, forever. The mind, after enough bruises, adjusted the scale.

You are also in the world. Important, yes. Loved, yes. But one among the many things that make a life, not the single thing a life is made of.

There is even a strange tenderness in this version. It lets people be human. It stops demanding that someone be a sun, and lets them be a person with their own weather, their own failures, their own limits. It frees them from my expectations, and frees me from the terror of depending on one light.

How I Love Now

I don’t hate anyone for it. I have simply stopped handing out the whole universe.

I love more carefully now. I keep a part of myself for myself, a room no one else holds the key to. I build other things to stand on: work that matters to me, friendships that don’t all run through one door, a relationship with my own company that doesn’t collapse when someone leaves it. I let people be what they are, important, but not everything.

Some will call this guarded. I call it load-bearing. A house with many beams can lose one and still stand. It is not less loving. It is just less breakable.

If You Are in That Quiet Place

If you are there right now, hollow, going through your days on schedule, wondering how someone who meant everything became just another face, know this.

The pain is real. You are not being dramatic, and you are not weak for having loved without reserve. That was never the mistake. It was the most generous thing you had.

And the world is real too, larger than that one person ever was. It is still out there, waiting, with people and places and mornings you have not met yet.

You are not broken.

You have just seen the full map.
