<p>There is a specific kind of exhaustion that has nothing to do with sleep. It’s the realization that you’ve been attending your own wake for years, nodding politely at the guests, and making small talk while your internal spark has long since been snuffed out.</p>

<p>The text poses a chilling question: <strong>What happens when Death finally arrives to collect a debt that has already been paid?</strong></p>

<h3>The Thief Who Found Nothing</h3>

<p>We often imagine Death as a thief—a shadow that slips into the room to steal our breath and our "spark." But there is a profound, albeit dark, irony in the idea of Death arriving only to find the vault empty.</p>

<p>When you’ve lived through seasons of profound burnout, grief, or total emotional erosion, you eventually reach a point of "spiritual bankruptcy." There is no breath left to steal because your lungs have been moving out of habit, not necessity. There is no spark to snuff because the fire went out during a storm decades ago.</p>

<h3>The Confusion of the Reaper</h3>

<p>I find the imagery of Death "kneeling" particularly moving. It suggests a moment of uncharacteristic humanity for an entity we usually view as indifferent. If Death is the ultimate witness to our end, what does it feel like to be a witness to a void?</p>

<p>To be "already dead" isn't necessarily a literal wish for the end; it is often a commentary on <strong>the performance of living.</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>It’s the career we no longer believe in.</li>

<li>It’s the relationships maintained out of obligation.</li>

<li>It’s the "people holding the funeral" through their pity, their labels, or their inability to see who we actually are beneath the surface.</li>
</ul>

<h3>The Laughter at the End</h3>

<p>The final lines are the most "savage" truth of all. The idea that Death might laugh because it’s the only one in on the joke. While the world sees a person walking, talking, and producing, Death sees the truth: the funeral began a long time ago.</p>

<p>Living in this state is a strange kind of purgatory. You are visible to everyone but felt by no one—not even yourself. It’s a soulful reminder that the greatest tragedy isn’t the end of life but the parts of us that die while we are still breathing.</p>

<p><strong>Is there a specific part of those lines that felt the most "real" to you today?</strong></p>
